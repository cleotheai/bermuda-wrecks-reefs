Wreck

L'Herminie

Reference only — details limited
History
Bermuda's most impressive warship wreck is the L'Herminie, a 60-gun French frigate that sank in 1838. This three-masted, wooden-hulled sailing vessel was en route from Havana to Bordeaux when it encountered a hurricane that damaged the rudder. The ship tried to make port, but a panicking crew — instigated by a drunken boatswain — abandoned ship before it went down. All 495 people aboard made it safely to shore. The ship sank in shallow water and 25 of its giant cannons remain on the white sand today. The French government offered a reward for salvage of the ship's 60 cannons; only 25 were ever recovered. The wreck site is surrounded by coral heads as tall as 30 feet but the ship remains relatively clean of marine growth. L'Herminie is a Protected Area Dive Site.
Current Condition
The wreck site is surrounded by coral heads as tall as 30 feet but the ship remains relatively clean of marine growth. Approximately 25 giant cannons remain scattered across white sand. The wreck site is relatively clean of marine growth compared to many other Bermuda wrecks.
