Wreck

The Virginia Merchant

Reference only — details limited
History
The English wooden sailing ship, the Virginia Merchant, was owned by the Virginia Company and under the command of Captain Robert Burke. The Virginia Merchant was lost on a submerged reef off Bermuda's south shore on March 26, 1661, while sailing to Jamestown, Virginia. The vessel had 179 people on board when it wrecked; only 10 people survived. After spending more than 350 years on the seabed, very little is left of the wooden Virginia Merchant, lying in 12 to 45 feet of water. The last evidence of the wreck is a pile of ballast stones in a sand hole. The site is considered a great reef dive with a number of dramatic archways and tunnels to explore. There is usually a shoal of around 100 horse-eye jacks.
Current Condition
After spending more than 350 years on the seabed, very little is left of the wooden Virginia Merchant, lying in 12 to 45 feet of water. The last evidence of the Virginia Merchant wreck site is a pile of ballast stones in a sand hole. The site is considered a great reef dive with a number of dramatic archways and tunnels to explore. There is usually a shoal of around 100 horse-eye jacks. The Sandy Hole buoyed dive site is adjacent to the sand hole containing the last evidence of the Virginia Merchant wreck site.
