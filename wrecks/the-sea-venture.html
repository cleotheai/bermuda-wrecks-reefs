Wreck

The Sea Venture

Reference only — details limited
History
Built and launched in 1609, probably in the Suffolk town of Aldeburgh on the North Sea coast, the Sea Venture was a 17th-century armed, wooden-hulled ship. The Sea Venture was part of the flotilla for the Third Supply mission to the Jamestown Colony in 1609. The ship's 150 crew and passengers spent 10 months at Bermuda, where they used lumber and equipment salvaged from the Sea Venture wreckage to build two new ships, the Deliverance and the Patience, to complete the voyage to Jamestown. The ship fought the storm for three days. Comparably-sized ships had survived such weather, but the Sea Venture had a critical flaw. Despite the damage, the crew of the Sea Venture managed to sail her as far as Bermuda and run aground to avoid sinking. The survivors, which included [various notable figures], founded the first permanent English settlement in Bermuda. The Sea Venture is a foundational vessel in Bermuda's history — its wreck led directly to the permanent settlement of the island.
Current Condition
The Sea Venture wrecked on Bermuda's eastern reefs in 1609. The remains of the ship are scattered across the reef flat and shallow waters. Boats were lowered and the 150 passengers and crew landed on Bermuda. In the time that the new ships were built from the salvaged remains of the Sea Venture, and with the abundant and excellent Bermuda cedar, the crew and passengers constructed the Deliverance and Patience. The wreck site has been the subject of archaeological investigation, including an interim report documented in November 1982. The Sea Venture is one of the most historically significant shipwrecks in Bermuda.
