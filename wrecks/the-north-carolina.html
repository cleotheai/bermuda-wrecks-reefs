Wreck

The North Carolina

Reference only — details limited
History
A classic sunken sailing ship, the North Carolina's 205- to 250-foot-long English iron hull sank on New Year's Day, 1880, en route from Bermuda (some accounts say Charleston to New York). The vessel was an iron-hulled English barque that measured 205 feet long and displaced 533 tons. The ill-fated ship struck the reef off the western portion of the island. The anchor broke free and crashed through the hull, tearing a huge hole and sending the vessel straight to the seabed. The North Carolina appeared in the Bermuda Sun Unprotected Historic Wrecks List submitted by the Receiver of Wreck in October 1977.
Current Condition
The wreck of the North Carolina lies approximately 8.5 miles west and five miles south of Gibbs Hill Lighthouse. Today she sits upright on a sandy seabed in 25 to 45 feet of water. The anchor broke free and crashed through the hull, tearing a huge hole and sending the vessel straight to the seabed. Some accounts claim a large piece of the hull capsized over onto its starboard side. The wreck sits upright on a sandy seabed in 25 to 45 feet of water.
