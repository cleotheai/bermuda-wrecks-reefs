Wreck

The Caraquet

History
Built in 1943 by Burntisland Shipbuilding Company, Scotland, as a cable repair ship, the Hermes served as the tender Pharos for the Northern Lighthouse Board until 1969. When she was acquired by the Bermuda Maritime Museum Association in 1984, she was renamed The Hermes. Plans were to sink her as an artificial reef, but after a series of financial and legal setbacks, the project was abandoned. Finally, in 1985, the Bermuda Dive Association took over the venture. Stripped of all salvageable metals, she was meticulously cleaned and readied as a diver's attraction. On December 17, 1985, she was finally towed to her final resting place one mile off Horseshoe Bay. Filled with explosives, she sank in less than two minutes, settling upright in 80 feet of water. Today, she rests as one of Bermuda's most popular dive sites, offering a breathtaking glimpse into maritime history wrapped in vibrant marine life.

Current Condition
Sitting upright in 80 feet of crystal-clear water, the Hermes remains in remarkable condition, offering an exciting dive experience for all skill levels. Her superstructure sits just 40 feet below the surface, making her an ideal site for novice and experienced divers alike. Because she was extensively cleaned prior to sinking, her decks are wide open and safe to explore. Visitors can swim through the wheelhouse, check out the cargo holds, and marvel at the massive propeller still intact at the stern. The wreck is heavily encrusted in colorful corals and sponges, attracting a dazzling array of marine life, including large groupers, barracudas, and schools of reef fish. The interior offers easy penetration for certified cave or wreck divers, while the expansive exterior is perfect for photography. The currents can occasionally be strong, but the rewarding dive makes it a must-see for anyone exploring Bermuda's underwater world.
