The Stonewall
Reference Only
Description
His wooden barque built in the 17th Century remains unidentified, but the site is known as the Stonewall wreck because, when discovered, the ballast stretched across the sand in a tight, high and elongated pile (like a stone wall in a field). The site was first registered by Harry Cox in the 1950s. It is lying in a sand hole and located to the west of the island just off a false cut immediately to the east of Western Blue Cut. All that remains of the vessel are the ballast as well as lower wooden timbers stretching across a sand hole between two areas of patch reef. 32°21'32.50"N, 64°54'10.80"W 16.3 feet (5 meters)
