Wreck

The Blanche King

History
The Blanche King sank in December 1920 en route from Norfolk to Bermuda. It was carrying a cargo of coal when the classic American schooner became stranded on the reefs off Bermuda's southern shoreline and sank into a sand hole in 35 feet of water. The ship had an iron frame, wooden sides and two decks, four masts and, distinctly, a huge drop keel — the keel could be retracted into the ship, significantly reducing the minimum allowable depth and making the ship useful for taking coal up shallow rivers to timber mills.
Current Condition
The wreck consists of extensively degraded remaining sections of the iron hull, with wood remains attached to the worn metal frames of the mid-decks (the deck's upper part is completely gone). There is evidence of the centerboard trunk still in place amidships and metal cable and rigging are strewn over and around the wreck and reef. The capstan (winch) is at the front of the ship and some ballast is exposed at the rear.
Statistics
Type:Classic American schooner (iron frame, wooden sides, four masts, drop keel)
Built:1887 in Maine, U.S.
Sank:December 1920 — stranded on reefs off Bermuda's southern shoreline en route from Norfolk to Bermuda, carrying coal
Location:32°16'27.8"N, 64°58'55.2"W
Depth:35 feet (10.7 m) — sank into a sand hole
Length:192 feet (58 m) / Width: 42 feet
Tonnage:1,156 tons displacement
Status / Protection:Protected by law — no fishing within 300 meters. Classified as an Open wreck and buoyed under the Bermuda Dive Sites program.
